📚 What's Covered in This Legal Guide?
1. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) and Indian Penal Code (IPC)

The introduction of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, marked a major change in India's criminal law framework. The BNS replaced the IPC for offences governed by the new law, subject to applicable commencement and transitional provisions.

This section focuses on understanding both the new provisions and the corresponding provisions of the former IPC.

Topics include:

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023
Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860
BNS vs IPC section comparisons
Theft, cheating, criminal breach of trust, and other offences
Punishments, bail classification, and relevant legal provisions

Popular legal references:

Section 380 IPC – Theft in a dwelling house
Section 420 IPC – Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property
Section 305 BNS – Theft in a dwelling house, etc.
Section 318 BNS – Cheating

Note: The correct BNS provision depends on the facts and legal elements of the offence. For example, Section 318(4) BNS addresses cheating that dishonestly induces delivery of property or certain related acts. Always verify the applicable provision against the official statutory text.

2. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) and Criminal Procedure

The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC), subject to applicable transitional provisions.

Explore legal topics relating to criminal procedure, including:

Arrest, investigation, and criminal trials
Bail and anticipatory bail
Police procedures and court proceedings
Important BNSS provisions and their CrPC counterparts
3. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) and Indian Evidence Act

The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA), replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, subject to applicable transitional provisions.

This section covers:

Relevant provisions of the BSA and former Evidence Act
Admissibility and relevance of evidence
Documentary and electronic evidence
Important evidence law principles and judicial decisions
4. Civil, Contract and Commercial Laws

Indian law extends beyond criminal legislation. This reference library also covers important civil and commercial statutes, including:

Indian Contract Act, 1872
Specific Relief Act, 1963
Sale of Goods Act, 1930
Indian Partnership Act, 1932

These resources can help students and legal professionals revise statutory provisions and understand fundamental legal concepts.

5. Domestic Violence, Family Law and Personal Laws

Find references to important legislation concerning domestic violence, marriage, succession, and family disputes.

Key statutes include:

Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005
Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
Hindu Succession Act, 1956
Family Courts Act, 1984

The Domestic Violence Act coverage includes provisions such as Section 29 (Appeal), Section 31 (Penalty for breach of protection order), and related procedural provisions.

Court judgments play an essential role in interpreting legislation and understanding how legal principles apply in practice.

Supreme Court judgments on criminal law
